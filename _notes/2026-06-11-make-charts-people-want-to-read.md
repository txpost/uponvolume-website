I’m stealing this from Paul Graham.

His version is make something people want.

If I take that version and adapt it to my needs, the north star becomes make charts people want to read.

I’ve been reading books and studying Other Peoples Charts for the past year or so and it’s unreal how bad some of the charts are.

Before we can start to define what a good chart is, we have to define what a bad chart is. We need to identify the problem before we try to provide a solution.

Here’s what I’m seeing in bad charts:
- Small text
- Poor context
- Hard to read lines
- Dark background
- Poor or no labels
- Text that is separate from the chart yet still describing the chart
- Too much information / trying to make too many points / unfocused
- No fun

What I think a good chart should look like:
- Large text
- Enough context so that the user instantly grasps what the chart is trying to say
- Clear lines that are easy to read
- Light background
- Sufficient labels
- On-chart annotations
- Sufficient/focused information to get a single point across
- Emits a sense of fun (emojis, graphics, conversational text)

I should be able to look at a chart and get the jist of it in a few seconds.

For multiple images, I should be able to arrow through the images in an image viewer and get the whole picture without having to read further analysis text.

I think a good barometer for the best charts is that I should want to print the chart off and frame it on my wall.

I think a chart style guide would go a long way. Not every chart needs to be the same person to person but I think it’s important to find your own style and taste and whatever that style is it needs to align with charts that people want to read.

There’s a YCombinator article about how to design a better pitch deck. It has three rules:
1. Make it legible
2. Make it simple
3. Make it obvious

I can take those rules and apply them to charts. I can think of each chart as a slide in a pitch deck.

How does make charts people want to read work as a personal motto? Simple. It’s the driving force behind everything I’m trying to do. If I make charts people want to read, then everything follows.

If I focus on making charts people want to read, it means:
- I’m getting better at chart pattern recognition
- I’m making content that people want
- I’m thinking clearer
- I’m doing Deliberate Practice instead of aimlessly looking at charts and learning about new setups
- My charts database grows
- I get better at trading
- I become the authority on making charts people want to read because nobody else is focused on this as far as I can tell
- I help other people learn how to make money in stocks
